How Decision Check works

Understand the portfolio first. Test the opportunity next.

01 · Portfolio Insider Agent

Describe current coins/tokens, stablecoins and Futures positions, then define your next plan. The agent computes NAV, weights, average cost, unrealized PnL and two risk limits. Your target asset does not have to be an existing holding.

02A · Wyckoff Agent

After Portfolio Insider is completed, this agent analyzes price/volume context for the planned asset. It checks trading ranges, ordered events and confirmation conditions for the selected horizon. Missing evidence is never forced into TRUE.

02B · SMC Agent

Runs independently of Wyckoff on the same validated dataset. It examines structure, liquidity sweeps and still-valid price zones. A bullish/bearish bias is distinguished from meeting every condition of a specific setup.

One decision, three perspectives and explicit reasons.

Where do I start?

Open Portfolio Insider Agent, describe your portfolio and plan, then select Analyze plan. Step 03 unlocks the continuation to Wyckoff–SMC with your chosen asset, product and profile. The market-agent tabs on the landing page show the workflow; they are not independent entry points before intake.

Can I hold OKB and USDT but test buying BNB?

Yes. Existing holdings and the planned asset are separate. Portfolio Insider includes all listed assets/positions in portfolio risk while testing how a new BNB purchase changes available cash and loss tolerance. Wyckoff and SMC analyze the asset in your plan, not an automatic scan of every coin.

Where do Wyckoff and SMC data come from?

Market analysis requests public Binance data for the planned Spot or USD-M Futures product. Closed candles, sufficient history, interval continuity and duplicate/gap checks must pass first. Cycle uses monthly/weekly/daily data; swing uses daily/H4/H1; intraday uses H4/H1/M15. An unavailable source or insufficient history yields incomplete data, without changing venues or substituting Spot for Futures. Other venue adapters are not supported yet.

How are cost, PnL and accepted loss treated?

Spot unrealized PnL = quantity × (current price − average cost). Linear Futures use entry and long/short direction. Per-asset accepted loss is a percentage of cost/entry, converted into a corresponding price. Margin is not counted twice in NAV. This is analysis of self-reported balances, not full ledger, fee or funding reconciliation. No stop order is placed.

How do the two risk limits differ?

The decision budget uses the original proposed investment and that decision’s accepted loss percentage. The cycle limit uses a selected NAV anchor and the whole portfolio after the plan. Both constrain the investment amount; the anchor does not automatically reset after losses or DCA. Lowering average cost does not erase existing losses.

How do the three agents work together?

Portfolio Insider establishes the portfolio state and plan scope first. Wyckoff and SMC receive the same snapshot but neither takes the other’s conclusion as input. The downstream check combines market conditions with portfolio limits for the requested amount. Each leg exposes validity and pass/fail; missing data or scope mismatches remain inconclusive. Futures margin and liquidation safety are still unverified.

What is TapeOut’s current role?

A local three-leg Boolean simulation has been implemented and tested across all 64 combinations of six validity/pass bits. It is not a deployed TapeOut circuit. Next: finalize rules, numeric encoding and bit bounds, build comparisons for loss budgets and NAV floors, measure cost and verify the design. Current results are not onchain proof.

What would X Layer record?

The planned direction is to deploy verified Decision Check circuits and record necessary references on X Layer. Records would distinguish inputs, rule version, circuit identity and read block/transaction where applicable. This workflow currently deploys no new circuit and sends no transaction. Full private portfolios are not published onchain.

Can I rerun a result?

Export a JSON record containing inputs and a report. Retaining the same data and code/rule version allows deterministic comparison. Changing the investment amount creates a new scenario rather than editing the original outcome. A hash identifies data; it does not authenticate complete account history or the correctness of a market interpretation.

What comes next?

Refine the three agents’ explanations and test more real-world cases; improve source coverage and report comparison; standardize quantitative inputs to design, test and deploy TapeOut circuits on X Layer. Private account synchronization is later work. The current product analyzes decisions and does not execute trades.
